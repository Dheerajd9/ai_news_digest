PRIYA: On October 19, 1987, the Dow Jones plunged 22.6 percent in a single day—the worst percentage crash in Wall Street history. The sell-off was accelerated by early computer programs automatically executing massive sell orders simultaneously. To ensure that kind of uncontrolled panic never repeats, global exchanges introduced circuit breakers. Think of a circuit breaker like the electrical fuse box in your home: when an electrical surge threatens to overheat the wiring and start a fire, the fuse automatically trips and cuts the power. In financial markets, if an index falls by 7 percent, 13 percent, or 20 percent, trading is temporarily halted for 15 to 45 minutes, or for the entire day. It gives investors, algorithms, and brokers time to pause, verify facts, and stop algorithmic panic from wiping out household savings.
