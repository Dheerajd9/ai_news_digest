KARTIK: That leads us to our educational concept this week: the Closing Auction Session on stock exchanges. Priya, what exactly is a Closing Auction Session, and why should regular investors understand it?
PRIYA: During the standard trading day, share prices fluctuate continuously as buy and sell orders match second by second. But in the final 15 to 30 minutes before the market closes, massive institutional trades arrive. If those enormous orders hit the order book all at once, a few institutional players could push a stock's price up or down artificially right at the closing bell. To eliminate that manipulation risk, exchanges halt continuous trading and run a Closing Auction Session.
KARTIK: How does the auction settle the price?
PRIYA: For roughly fifteen minutes, the exchange pools all incoming buy and sell orders without executing them immediately. Then, an automated algorithm identifies one single clearing price—the exact price level where the maximum volume of shares can be executed. That price becomes the official daily closing price for the stock. It ensures that mutual funds, pension funds, and everyday retail investors receive a fair, transparent benchmark that cannot be manipulated by late-day order swings.
