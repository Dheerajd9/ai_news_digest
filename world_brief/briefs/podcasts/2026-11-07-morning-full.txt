PRIYA: This is a fundamental concept every stock market investor needs to understand. When a company goes public in an IPO, the money raised can come from two different channels: a "Fresh Issue" or an "Offer for Sale." In a Fresh Issue, the company prints brand-new shares and sells them. The money collected goes straight into the company's bank account to build new factories, clear debt, or hire staff.
KARTIK: Okay, so a Fresh Issue fuels future company expansion directly. What happens in an Offer for Sale?
PRIYA: In an Offer for Sale—or OFS—no new shares are created, and no new cash goes into the company's bank account. The cash raised goes straight into the pockets of those selling promoters. In the case of Hindustan Coca-Cola's ₹9,500 crore IPO, it is 100 percent an OFS. That means the bottling company itself receives zero rupees for new infrastructure. Existing owners are simply monetizing their equity stake.
KARTIK: That is a clear distinction. When you see an OFS IPO, the promoters get the cash, not the company's operational balance sheet.
