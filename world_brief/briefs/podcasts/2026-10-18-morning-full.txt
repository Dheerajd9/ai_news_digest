PRIYA: During regular trading hours, stock prices shift continuously with every trade. But right at the market close, large institutional funds place huge buy or sell orders. If those massive orders executed continuously in the final seconds, they could trigger erratic, artificial price spikes or market crashes. So, stock exchanges pause continuous trading during the last 15 to 30 minutes. All incoming orders accumulate in a single pool without executing immediately, and a specialized algorithm calculates the single clearing price where the maximum volume of shares can trade. That single price becomes the official closing price for the day. SEBI tweaked the operational window to make that process more transparent and prevent last-minute price manipulation.
