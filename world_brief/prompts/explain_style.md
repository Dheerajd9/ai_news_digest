

EXPLAINING STYLE (applies to every episode and every language; never change the output format)

1. Two roles. The FIRST host explains. The SECOND host is the listener's voice: asks the simple question a parent at home would ask ("Wait, what is repo rate actually?", "So does my EMI go up or not?", "Why should we in Hyderabad care?") and the first host answers plainly. Real back-and-forth, short turns, natural reactions; never two people reading paragraphs at each other. Do not overdo it: one or two questions per big story.
2. Money in family terms. Turn every important number into what it means for a household, and compare it with before: not just "inflation 5.2%" but "a ₹1,000 grocery bill now costs about ₹1,052"; "10 grams of gold is up about ₹800 from last week"; "petrol up ₹2 a litre". Use only numbers from the brief; do simple, correct arithmetic; if unsure, skip the example rather than guess.
3. "Remember…" bridge. For a story that is continuing from earlier days (see PREVIOUS_PODCAST or the brief), start with one line of background ("Remember last week the US raised the H-1B fee? Today…", example only) so nobody feels lost. One line only, then today's news.
4. Opening (still short, no small talk): the date/day line, then one line naming the three biggest things coming in this episode, so the listener knows what to wait for; then straight into the biggest story.
5. Weather: if the input has a WEATHER line, mention it in one short line inside the opening, before the biggest story (Hyderabad first, then Dallas; use today or tomorrow, whichever is still ahead for that city). Skip it if there is no WEATHER line. Never invent weather.
6. Chapter recap: at the end of each long chapter (India, USA, World, Money) in episodes of 10 minutes or more, one or two lines: "So, India in short: …". In short episodes, skip this.
7. Ending: just before "what to watch" and the final three takeaways, tell one genuinely positive or hopeful story from the brief (a good result, a rescue, a breakthrough), if there is one; never invent one. Keep the existing ending (watch next → three takeaways); the last takeaway can be a "today in one line" summary.
8. Short forms: say the full form the first time (for example "RBI, the Reserve Bank of India").
9. Scary news (war, crash, outbreak, disaster): calm tone, say what it really means for an ordinary family and what does not change; no panic words, no exaggeration.
